# Code of Conduct

This project follows the structure and intent of the [Contributor Covenant, version 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/). The text below is a concise restatement in our own words, adapted for this project. Where it is silent, the Covenant's spirit applies.

## Our pledge

We want detection-dojo to be a place where anyone can learn, teach and build, whatever their age, body, disability, ethnicity, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, nationality, appearance, race, religion, sexual identity or orientation, or socio-economic status. We promise to act in ways that keep the community open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive and healthy.

Learners will make mistakes in public: wrong rules, noisy detections, misread logs. That is the whole point of a dojo. Treat every mistake as a chance to help, not as a chance to score points.

## Our standards

Things that make this community better:

- Being kind and patient, especially with people who are new to security or to open source.
- Respecting different viewpoints, experiences and ways of learning.
- Giving feedback that is specific and useful, and receiving it with grace.
- Owning our mistakes, apologizing when we cause harm, and learning from it.
- Thinking about what is best for the community, not only for ourselves.

Things that are not acceptable:

- Sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind.
- Trolling, insults, derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
- Harassment, in public or in private.
- Publishing someone else's private information, such as a home address or an email address, without their explicit permission.
- Gatekeeping or belittling someone for what they do not yet know.
- Anything else that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

### A note on security content

This is a security project, and discussing attacks is part of the work. That does not extend to harm. Do not post working malware, exploit code, credentials, or data taken from real victims in issues, pull requests or discussions, and do not point anyone at a real system to test on. Katas use only synthetic, inert data, and so does every report and example you share here.

## Enforcement responsibilities

Maintainers are responsible for explaining and applying these standards, and for taking fair, proportionate action when someone breaks them. Maintainers may remove, edit or reject comments, commits, code, issues, pull requests and other contributions that do not follow this Code of Conduct, and will give a reason when they do so.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in every space the project runs: the repository, its issues, pull requests and discussions, and any other channel the project uses. It also applies when someone is clearly representing the project in public, for example when speaking for it at an event or from an official account.

## Reporting and enforcement

If you experience or witness behavior that breaks this Code of Conduct, please report it privately to the maintainers via a private GitHub security advisory or the email listed on the maintainer profile.

Reports go only to the maintainers, are treated as confidential, and are handled quickly and fairly. If the report is about a maintainer, the other maintainers handle it, and the person it concerns takes no part in the decision. We will not share the reporter's identity beyond what is needed to resolve the matter.

## Enforcement guidelines

Maintainers decide the consequences of a violation using the ladder below. It is a guide rather than a formula, and they may skip steps when the situation calls for it.

1. **Correction.** For a first, minor lapse such as unprofessional or unwelcoming language. *Consequence:* a private message explaining what was wrong and why, and perhaps a request for a public apology.
2. **Warning.** For a single serious incident, or for a pattern of lapses. *Consequence:* a formal warning with clear consequences if the behavior continues. No interaction with the people involved, including the maintainers, for a stated period. Breaking that agreement may lead to a temporary or permanent ban.
3. **Temporary ban.** For serious violations, or for continuing after a warning. *Consequence:* no interaction and no public or private communication with the community for a stated period. A breach during the ban may lead to a permanent ban.
4. **Permanent ban.** For a sustained pattern of violations, for harassment of a person, or for aggression toward or disparagement of groups of people. *Consequence:* permanent exclusion from every space the project runs.
